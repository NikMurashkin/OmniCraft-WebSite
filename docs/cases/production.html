Batch Production Accounting in a Leathercraft Workshop ​

A step-by-step walkthrough of a 10-piece leather clutch batch: material procurement, recipe card, inventory write-off, and net profit (P&L).

Introductory Note on Pricing

All material, hardware, and chemical prices in this guide are provided as practical reference benchmarks for a cohesive calculation model. Every craftsman works with distinct supplier rates, regional shipping costs, and hourly wage targets. The primary goal of this case study is to demonstrate the structural relationships, the recipe card breakdown, the inventory write-off sequence, and the exact mathematical reconciliation of the Profit and Loss statement (P&L) in OmniCraft ERP, into which you can easily plug your own actual supplier receipts and invoices.

For this practical walkthrough, we take the standard scenario of an independent leathercraft artisan or small boutique studio.

How Fixture Amortization is Allocated to a Batch

The lifespan of the custom beech shaping block for this model is 20 clutches (2 batches of 10 units). A batch of 10 units consumes exactly half (1/2) of the tool's lifespan: 3,000 ₽ ÷ 20 = 150 ₽ per unit, or 1,500 ₽ ($18) for the entire 10-piece batch. This figure is automatically incorporated into the recipe card and accumulated in the workshop's equipment replacement reserve with every completed sale.

Click «Process». The software executes three operations simultaneously:

  1. Inventory Receipt:
    Logs physical stock into inventory with exact units of measure (sq. dm, pieces, kits).
  2. Moving Average Cost (MAC):
    Establishes the moving average unit cost for each stock item across deliveries.
  3. Startup Liability Tracking:
    Registers a corresponding entry in Cash Flow under «Owner Capital Deposit» totaling 20,000 ₽ (plus 3,000 ₽ for equipment capitalized in Step 1). The workshop now formally records a 23,000 ₽ total liability to repay this invested startup capital after the batch is sold.

Step 3: Cutting Yield (Efficiency Rate) and Waste Accounting ​

Each natural hide possesses irregular contours, variable fiber density across topographical zones, and edge characteristics (flanks, neck wrinkles, healed marks).

When assembling structural zip clutches, exterior panels must be cut exclusively from the dense bend (butt) area to avoid deformation under zipper tension. Consequently, the material efficiency rate (yield) is 80%:

Cutting Balance for the Batch ​

  • Purchased Exterior Hide Area150 sq. dm (55 ₽/sq. dm = 8,250 ₽)
  • Net Cut Area for 10 Clutches120 sq. dm (12 sq. dm per clutch)
  • Technological Offcuts (Scrap)30 sq. dm (20% trim rate)
Cutting Economics
True Cost per Usable Sq. Dm at 80% Yield
When offcuts are discarded:55 ₽ ÷ 0.80 = 68.75 ₽ / sq. dm(15 sq. dm gross usage per clutch)
When retaining usable scrap:12 sq. dm × 55 ₽ = 660 ₽(30 sq. dm worth 2,100 ₽ stay in stock)

The 30 sq. dm of secondary scraps are retained on the workshop balance sheet as «Usable Scrap Inventory» for small accessories (keychains, card sleeves, watch straps).

Scrap Accounting Options & Warehouse Inventory Reconciliation

When offcuts are not used for smaller goods: If your workshop does not produce keychains or cardholders and belly scraps are discarded, do not capitalize them as usable scrap. Instead, enter the gross consumption rate into the recipe card: 150 sq. dm ÷ 10 pcs = 15 sq. dm per clutch (825 ₽ instead of 660 ₽), fully allocating the entire hide cost to the batch.

Reconciling scrap variances via Inventory Act: Natural hides vary in usable area, and cutting yield can fluctuate across batches. If a variance gradually accumulates between the software's recorded stock and the physical scraps on your shelf, open the Warehouse tab, launch an «Inventory Act», and write off the shortage or record surplus stock in a single step — the system automatically reconciles your inventory asset value.

    The batch appears on the active Workbench view as «Batch #101: Zip Clutch (10 pcs)».

Shortage Guard: Preventing "Blind" Batch Production

Before cutting valuable hides, OmniCraft automatically audits current stock availability for the entire planned batch. If even a single component is insufficient (for example, having only 8 perimeter zippers instead of 10), the software displays a shortage alert detailing the exact missing materials and highlighting the deficit. The maker can immediately log a quick purchase order or adjust the batch quantity to match available stock.

Production proceeds through distinct operations: rough cutting ➔ edge skiving ➔ card slot pocket assembly ➔ wooden block zipper mounting ➔ pricking and hand saddle stitching ➔ 3-stage edge burnishing.

Upon completion, clicking «Complete» transfers 10 finished clutches directly to the Products Showcase inventory at a capitalized balance of 4,340 ₽ each (total finished goods asset value: 43,400 ₽).

Step 6: Sales Realization & Reconciled P&L Statement ​

The batch of 10 clutches is sold through direct craft channels and bespoke commissions at the retail price of 8,900 ₽. Over a three-week window, all 10 clutches are delivered and logged in the Sales module.

Troubleshooting & Batch Pitfalls ​

What if a pattern piece or leather panel is ruined during cutting or punching?

If an exterior panel is damaged and needs recutting, navigate to the "Raw Materials" tab and record an inventory write-off for the additional leather area noting "Scrap piece in batch ORD-0001". Moving Average Cost (MAC) will absorb the expense, ensuring adjusted batch unit costs reflect real-world workshop yield without distorting physical inventory balances.

What if actual consumption of adhesives, thread, or finishes exceeds recipe estimates?

In OmniCraft ERP, auxiliary shop consumables are handled either via direct volume deduction or as an overhead percentage. If extra edge paint or cement was consumed, deduct it on the "Raw Materials" tab and bump the product's overhead allocation percentage in the "Calculator" for future runs to cover actual shop appetite.

What if the client requests different hardware while the batch is already In Production?

On the "Workbench" tab, click the "Cancel Task" button (red cross icon). All reserved materials, zippers, and fittings immediately return to raw inventory. Update the recipe in the "Calculator" (switch sliders or zipper colors) and relaunch the run.
