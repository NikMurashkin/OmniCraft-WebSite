Finished Goods: Showroom Inventory and FIFO Batches ​

In bespoke craftsmanship and small-batch production, finished goods sitting on the showroom shelf represent materialized working capital. Until an item is delivered into the customer's hands, every seam, polished edge, and coat of protective oil ties up real money: the purchase cost of consumed materials, the value of the craftsman's labor hours, and an allocated share of workshop overhead.

When showroom inventory is managed haphazardly or tracked in a generic spreadsheet, the maker inevitably loses financial clarity: older stock is sold at contemporary inflated rates, batch cost integrity collapses, and the storefront misleads buyers with out-of-date stock levels.

The "Finished Goods" tab in OmniCraft ERP serves as the central showroom inventory registry. It automatically receives products completed on the Workbench, tracks actual on-hand stock, preserves frozen FIFO cost batches, and computes recommended retail prices for sale.

Display Modes and Warehouse Navigation ​

The finished goods interface is engineered for instant stock assessment and rapid inventory reconciliation. The top toolbar provides streamlined workspace controls:

  • Warehouse Rows (Default layout): a compact operational table featuring square 1:1 thumbnails, exact shelf quantities, actual weighted-average batch cost, calculated retail price, and quick action buttons for every item. Engineered for high-volume inventory management.
  • Product Cards (Grid layout): a spacious visual showroom gallery featuring large photographs and detailed parameters. Ideal for periodic visual stock audits, catalog presentations to clients, and verifying public storefront formatting.

Warehouse Filtering and Instant Search ​

The central toolbar features a three-way status selector:

  • "All": displays the complete roster of active workshop designs, including items with zero shelf stock (allowing you to quickly launch production or edit recipes).
  • "In Stock": isolates items that are physically present in the workshop right now (stock > 0).
  • "Archived": reveals discontinued models or seasonal items temporarily withdrawn from active circulation.

The search input in the right corner includes an instant clear button and filters items dynamically by name without reloading the view.

Anatomy of an Inventory Product Row ​

Every row in the finished goods registry contains a comprehensive set of manufacturing, quantitative, and financial indicators:

  • Product Thumbnail (1:1): a high-resolution square preview automatically cropped from the bill of materials recipe or web showcase photos.
  • Model Name: the full product title with quick access to viewing and editing.
  • Stock Badge: a color-coded status chip. When stock is available, a green indicator appears (e.g., "In Stock: 4 pcs"); at zero stock, a neutral gray chip reads "Out of Stock".
  • Lifetime Manufacturing Counter: the "Total Manufactured: [X]" metric, recording the total volume of units crafted since the inception of the workshop database.
  • Special Status Chips:
    • "Archived" — the linked recipe has been moved to the archive;
    • "No Recipe" — the item was added directly to stock without a preconfigured manufacturing recipe.

The right side of the row houses quick-action icon buttons: edit or create manufacturing recipe, open the inventory adjustment modal, archive the product, or restore it from the archive.

Cost Accounting Mechanics: Dual Cost Pricing and FIFO Batches ​

A hallmark of professional workshop management is the clear separation between theoretical current manufacturing costs and the historical batch cost of existing inventory.

On each product row, OmniCraft ERP simultaneously computes and displays two independent cost figures:

  1. Actual Stock Cost (Weighted Moving Average): the actual cost of the specific physical units currently sitting on your shelves, computed from the frozen batch costs of units completed on the Workbench.
  2. Calculated Recipe Cost (Theoretical Current): the projected cost to manufacture one unit "right now," based on current raw material inventory prices and active workshop labor rates.

First-In, First-Out (FIFO) in Real Workshop Practice ​

To relieve inventory during sales or write-offs, OmniCraft ERP applies the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method. Inventory batches are depleted in the exact chronological order of their production.

Practical Example: Shielding Profit Margins from Material Inflation

Consider a real-world scenario in a leather workshop:

  1. One month ago, a craftsman produced three classic belts. Materials were inexpensive, freezing the actual cost of each belt at $30. The belts were placed on the shelf.
  2. Today, the leather supplier increased prices by 40%. The Calculator indicates that producing the same belt right now costs $45.
  3. A client enters the studio and purchases one belt.

If the software automatically averaged costs across time or repriced existing belts using today's recipe rates, profit reporting would be distorted. OmniCraft ERP preserves batch history: the belt sold is deducted from the oldest batch at exactly $30. This guarantees clean management accounting and accurate net profit calculation.

Recommended Retail Price and Sales Channels ​

Directly below the cost indicators, the system displays the calculated retail price. OmniCraft ERP establishes this figure via two synchronized mechanisms:

  • Automatic Markup Calculation: the cost price is multiplied by the designated markup percentage. Custom markup set within the product's bill of materials takes precedence. If no individual markup is defined, the global workshop markup from Space Settings (default 100%) is applied.
  • Fixed Price Priority from Sales Channels: if a fixed price is configured in the sales channels table (for instance, dedicated pricing for the "Website" channel or an external marketplace), the software surfaces that approved retail price instead.

This safeguard protects the craftsman from accidentally selling items below operational profitability during sudden raw material price surges.

Direct Stock Intake (Bypassing the Workbench) ​

In daily workshop operations, products do not always enter inventory strictly through the Workbench manufacturing queue. Certain workflows require bringing finished stock directly onto the books.

The left action toolbar features the "Receive Goods" button, opening a modal dialog for direct inventory intake.

  1. Product Selection:
    Choose a catalog item or enter a name. If that name already exists in the catalog, the batch is added to the existing item.
  2. Intake Quantity:
    The number of finished units being added directly to warehouse inventory.
  3. Unit Cost Price:
    Lock in the unit cost for this independent FIFO inventory batch.
  4. Retail Price
    If you fill in “Retail Price (RRP, optional)”, the website channel price is saved with the batch. Use the storefront editor to control publication.

Model for a stock item

In “Receive Finished Goods”, you can set “Product model” for a new or existing item without a recipe. Items with a recipe use the model selected in the Calculator.

Standard Intake Scenarios: ​

  1. Entering Opening Balances: when onboarding onto OmniCraft ERP with pre-existing stock crafted prior to software adoption.
  2. Consignment and Partner Goods: taking in items crafted by partner artisans or collaborative studios for joint retail distribution.
  3. Showroom Sample Returns: returning display models from exhibitions, trade fairs, or photography studios if they were previously written off.

Accounting Integrity: Zero Raw Material and Cash Impact

Direct finished goods intake registers a new incoming batch in showroom inventory, but does not consume raw materials from warehouse stock and generates no cash outflow entries in the Cash Flow Ledger. It is a pure asset recognition transaction.

Inventory Adjustments: Reconciling Discrepancies ​

Physical inventory occasionally diverges from software records: an item might sustain shelf damage, be misplaced, or surface during a seasonal studio cleanup. To reconcile these discrepancies, every product row and card features an "Inventory Adjustment" button.

The system supports two adjustment types:

  1. "Record Surplus (+)": increases physical shelf count. The "Unit Cost" field defaults to the current recipe cost from the Calculator, but the maker can manually override this figure. The software logs a new FIFO batch.
  2. “Write off loss (-)” deducts the specified quantity from the oldest batches first. If the shortage exceeds current stock, the adjustment is rejected and batch quantities are preserved.

Do Not Substitute Sales with Loss Write-Offs!

Writing off a loss removes the product without logging revenue. If an item was handed to a customer, gifted to a loyal patron, or discounted at 100%, always record it through the "Sales" tab with a $0 retail price. This ensures the batch cost is accurately logged in Profit and Loss reports as a commercial marketing expense rather than an unaccounted warehouse shortage.

Product Lifecycle: Archiving and Capital Protection ​

A workshop's product catalog evolves continuously: older designs are retired, and seasonal collections yield to new lines. OmniCraft ERP provides structured archiving alongside rigorous capital safeguards.

Archiving a Product ​

To hide an item from the active inventory roster:

  1. Click the "Archive product" button (archive icon) on the right side of the item row.
  2. Confirm the archive action. If a production order is open, complete the batch or cancel the order first.
  3. Select “Archived” to find the item. Its stock and batches are preserved and can still be sold. Restore the item before starting a new production order.

Recipe Specifications Are Preserved

When a product is archived, its linked recipe in the Calculator automatically moves to the "Archived Recipes" folder. Raw material consumption ratios, labor time benchmarks, and overhead coefficients remain intact. You can restore the design to active production at any time by clicking the restore icon.

Restoring and Deleting ​

In the "Archived" filter view, two actions are available:

  • Restore Product (unarchive icon): returns the item to the active inventory catalog and re-enables its recipe on the Workbench.
  • “Delete product” (trash icon) removes the item and linked recipe from the catalog. Completed transactions stay in the records.

Deletion requirements

You can delete a product when its stock balance is 0 pcs and it has no open production orders. If stock remains, record a sale or use inventory adjustment to write off an actual shortage.

Troubleshooting & Common Pitfalls ​

What should I do if physical shelf counts do not match inventory figures in the software?

Open the inventory adjustment on the item card and enter the difference in units. For a surplus, enter the unit cost of the new batch. Missing units are deducted from the oldest batches first.

Why won't the system allow deleting a product from the catalog?

Check the stock balance and production queue. Archiving hides the item from the active catalog while you sell the remaining stock. If a production order is open, complete or cancel it.

How can I restore an accidentally archived item?

Select “Archived” and click the restore button on the product card. The item returns to the active catalog with its batches and prices.
