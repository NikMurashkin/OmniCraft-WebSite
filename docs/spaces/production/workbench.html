Production: The Workbench & Manufacturing Queue ​

In craftsmanship and custom manufacturing, friction inevitably arises at the intersection of scheduling and active assembly: if materials are deducted from spreadsheets prematurely, warehouse stock reports misleading deficits for urgent orders; if materials are never reserved, multiple scheduled products compete for the exact same batch of leather, timber, or hardware. As a result, the workshop either halts work due to unexpected stockouts or buries liquid capital in excessive safety buffer stock.

The "Production" (Workbench) tab in OmniCraft ERP serves as an intelligent dispatching dashboard for your workshop. All batches scheduled from the "Calculator" tab converge here. The primary engineering strength of the Workbench is dynamic cascade planning without rigid warehouse stock locks.

Principle of Soft Material Reservation ​

Unlike rigid enterprise systems where scheduling an order permanently locks warehouse items, OmniCraft ERP employs the concept of a "soft reserve."

When clicking the "Start Production" button in the Calculator, raw materials are not physically deducted from warehouse stock. Instead, the application computes a dynamic virtual reserve in memory, evaluating material availability in real time.

This provides the craftsman with essential operational flexibility:

  • You can outline a production pipeline weeks in advance, even if key raw materials have not yet been delivered or are still in transit from suppliers.
  • Inventory records in the "Materials" tab remain clean, reflecting exact physical stock sitting on your workshop shelves.
  • If customer deadlines change suddenly, any batch can be rearranged in the queue or cancelled instantly without generating tedious return invoices.

Dynamic Queue and Cascade Prioritization ​

The Workbench queue operates on a top-down cascade resource allocation principle. The system evaluates current physical stock in the warehouse and allocates available quantities sequentially based on each order's numerical position in the queue.

Every order card in the active queue displays one of two real-time statuses:

  1. "✅ Secured with materials" — the warehouse holds sufficient free stock across all required components, factoring in the needs of all higher-priority orders in the list.
  2. "⚠️ Material deficit" — the application explicitly details which materials are missing alongside the exact shortage quantity in standard measurement units (sq ft, lin m, kg, pcs).
Dispatching Principle
Cascade Stock Allocation by Priority Rank
Free Available Stock=Physical Inventory−Sum of Reserves for Orders Higher in Queue

Practical Case: Managing Order Priority ​

Consider a realistic scenario in a leathercraft studio:

  • Active warehouse stock shows: 100 sq ft of vegetable-tanned leather.
  • Two orders are queued on the Workbench:
    1. Travel Duffel Bag (requires 80 sq ft of leather);
    2. Urban Daypack (requires 90 sq ft of leather).

If the Duffel Bag sits above the Daypack in the Workbench queue, the system allocates 80 sq ft to the Duffel Bag (Status: Secured with materials). For the Daypack, only 20 sq ft remains, prompting the status: Material deficit: Leather (70.0 sq ft).

If the Daypack client pays for expedited delivery, the craftsman clicks the "Move up in queue" (↑) arrow button on the Daypack card. The application recalculates stock availability from the top down: the Daypack now claims 90 sq ft (switching to green secured status), while the Duffel Bag drops below and enters deficit status (70.0 sq ft).

Master Craftsman Tip: Scheduling Ahead with Zero Stock

You can confidently schedule orders against expected incoming shipments. As soon as a purchasing invoice is recorded under the Materials tab, red deficit indicators across your Workbench automatically turn green without requiring manual re-entry.

Anatomy of a Production Order Card ​

Each order on the Workbench is rendered as an independent card equipped with comprehensive dispatching controls:

  • Queue Reordering Controls: Dedicated "Move up in queue" (↑) and "Move down in queue" (↓) arrow buttons. The up arrow is disabled for the first item; the down arrow is disabled for the last.
  • Order Information Block:
    • Product name derived from the specification sheet;
    • Batch quantity (e.g., “Quantity: 5 pcs”);
    • Exact date and time the order was queued;
    • Estimated batch cost (calculated from specification consumption norms and active warehouse prices);
    • Real-time material allocation status.
  • AI Order Assistant (spark icon button): Initiates an automated assessment of the production order. The AI assistant evaluates material readiness, compares estimated costs against batch volume, and provides recommendations on assembly sequencing.
  • "Cancel" Button: Opens a confirmation modal. Once confirmed, the order is removed from the Workbench, and its soft reserve of materials is instantly reallocated to lower-ranked orders.
  • "Complete" Button: Triggers the physical completion process, recording finished stock into inventory.

Finished Goods Release (Completing Production) ​

When a batch of goods is physically assembled, burnished, inspected, and approved for release, the craftsman clicks the "Complete" button.

At this exact millisecond, the application executes a single atomic transaction within SQLite:

  1. Physical Raw Materials Deduction: The system strictly deducts product components (usage_type == 'product'). Packaging components (mailer boxes, tissue wrap, tags) remain untouched in stock until the final sale. The inventory log records a deduction entry titled “Consumption: Production [Product] x[Quantity]”.
  2. Historical Cost Price Snapshot: The application calculates unit cost at the moment of completion using current moving average cost (MAC) valuations, bench time rates, and overhead multipliers. This unit cost figure is permanently frozen for this specific batch.
  3. Showcase Batch Entry (FIFO): The newly completed batch is booked into showroom inventory (the "Showcase" tab) under a dedicated record in finished_goods_batches referencing the original production order ID and frozen unit cost.
  4. Order Finalization: The order status changes to completed with a completion timestamp, removing it from the active Workbench queue.
  1. Deduct product raw materials:
    Record an inventory deduction log without touching packaging stock prematurely.
  2. Freeze historical unit cost:
    Permanently snapshot unit cost (MAC + labor + overheads) for this batch.
  3. Book finished goods batch:
    Add completed units to finished stock with independent FIFO batch tracking.
  4. Close production order:
    Record completion timestamp, mark status as done, and archive the workbench card.

Inventory Physics: Protecting Cost Valuation via First In, First Out (FIFO)

Suppose on Monday you manufactured 3 belts using leather purchased at an older wholesale price (batch cost: $25 per unit). On Friday, fresh leather arrived at a higher cost, and you manufactured 3 more identical belts (batch cost: $32 per unit).

Your finished goods inventory now holds 6 identical belts. When a customer purchases a belt via the Sales tab, the system will deduct units from the Monday batch at $25 first, and subsequently from the Friday batch at $32 following the First In, First Out (FIFO) method. Your financial profit report reflects authentic commercial margins without arbitrary cost averaging.

Continuous Operations: Tolerance for Negative Material Balances

OmniCraft ERP prioritizes real-world workshop continuity. If a craftsman forgot to enter yesterday's delivery invoice for adhesives or hardware into the software, the system will not block production completion. The raw material balance slides into a temporary technical negative, allowing completed goods to be booked into stock on time. When the invoice is entered later, inventory balances reconcile automatically.

Trial Version Limitations (Demo Mode) ​

The free trial version of the software includes a limit of 20 processed commercial transactions (sales receipts):

  • Once this threshold is reached, a "Trial Version Limit Reached" warning banner appears across the top of the Workbench.
  • The "Complete" button is disabled, shifting the Workbench into a view-only schedule.
  • However, reordering queue priorities via the up/down arrows and cancelling pending orders remain fully functional.
  • To remove the restriction, simply activate a lifetime license key or active subscription under Workspace Settings.
