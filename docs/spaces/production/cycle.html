Production Cycle: Craftsman's Step-by-Step Guide ​

In artisanal craft and manufacturing, success is rarely determined solely by manual bench skill; it relies on the disciplined connection between technological execution and financial accounting. When purchases are jotted down in a pocket notebook, recipes kept in memory, stock estimated by eye, and revenue spent directly from your pocket, a business inevitably falls victim to cash shortages and phantom profits.

In OmniCraft ERP, the manufacturing workflow operates as a continuous, end-to-end conveyor. Every physical operation in your workshop is immediately mirrored in the system ledger: raw intake sets inventory valuation, recipe templates calculate labor allocations, the workbench tracks batch scrap, and sales record net transaction margins into cash flow.

Physical Workshop Reference Example ​

To inspect how the production conveyor runs with real figures, we will walk through the entire cycle using a classic craft benchmark: "Saddle Leather Bifold Wallet":

  • Raw Materials: vegetable-tanned bovine leather (12.5 sq dm), braided waxed thread (4 m), edge dressing compound, and finishing wax.
  • Craftsman Labor: 2.5 hours of focused labor at a standard workshop rate of $15.00/hour.
  • Overhead Allocations: machine and hand tool depreciation, studio workspace rent, and power utilities.
  • Packaging Kit: rigid kraft gift box, tissue paper, and custom embossed product hangtag.
  • Batch Release Volume: a small batch of 5 completed units on the workbench.

Step 1. Raw Materials Intake (Warehouse) ​

Every handcrafted good begins with raw resources. Before cutting the first pattern, materials must be officially received into stock.

Navigate to the "Materials" tab (Warehouse) and click the "Batch Materials Purchase" action:

  1. Entering Invoice Lines: Specify the material name, intake volume, and unit purchase price from your supplier.
  2. Factoring Courier Freight: If shipping costs $10.00 for the entire parcel, enter this total into the delivery cost field. The software automatically distributes freight across all purchased line items proportional to their invoice value, absorbing shipping directly into inventory cost.
  3. Cash Flow Disbursement: Saving the purchase instantly creates an expense entry in the cash flow register (the "DDS" tab). No duplicate manual logging is required.

Moving Average Cost (MAC)

If your shelves already hold leather stock at $0.80 per sq dm and a new batch arrives at $1.00, the system automatically recalculates inventory value using the Moving Average Cost (MAC) method. All future deductions will draw from this realistic, balanced valuation.

Step 2. Engineering the Recipe Template (Calculator) ​

Once raw supplies are logged, you define the technical specifications for the product. Open the "Calculator" tab, switch to the "Recipe Database" mode, and click "Create Recipe":

  1. Core Parameters: Name your item ("Bifold Wallet (Cognac)") and enter the production standard—2.5 hours. The software multiplies this time by your workshop hourly rate ($15.00/h), reserving $37.50 in craftsman compensation.
  2. Bill of Materials (Deducted on Workbench): Add required supplies from your active inventory:
    • Vegetable-tanned leather: 12.5 sq dm (at active MAC warehouse valuation $0.90 = $11.25);
    • Waxed thread: 4 meters (at $0.20 = $0.80);
    • Edge finishes and sealants: standardized unit allowance $1.50.
  3. Overhead Allocation: Apply indirect expense coefficients. The system incorporates machine wear and rent shares (in our example, $4.20).
  4. Packaging Kit (Deferred Deduction): In the packaging sub-tab, select the presentation set: kraft box, tissue, and hangtag totaling $3.50.

Workshop Pro Tip: Scrap Yield Allowance

When cutting natural hides or hardwoods, edge scrap and natural blemishes are unavoidable. Never record pattern square footage without scrap margin. Always add a 15–20% scrap buffer to recipe raw material quantities so your unit cost reflects actual raw inventory consumption.

Decoupling Workbench Assembly and Packaging

Packaging materials are not deducted when assembling items on the workbench. Finished wallets may rest in inventory wrap for weeks before a buyer selects either standard kraft boxes or premium wooden gift chests. Packaging is included in recipes to determine complete retail pricing, but physical warehouse stock is deducted only when the sale is finalized.

Step 3. Batch Dispatch to the Workbench ​

With specifications established, you proceed to cut and assemble the production run.

  1. In the "Calculator" tab, switch to the "Batch Costing" mode.
  2. Search for the "Bifold Wallet (Cognac)" template and specify the batch volume: 5 units.
  3. Inventory Feasibility Check: The system verifies required resources (62.5 sq dm leather, 20 m thread) against live shelf stock. If stock is sufficient, the status displays a clear green confirmation indicator.
  4. Click "Launch to Production". The job moves to the workbench, reserving materials in the warehouse.

Step 4. Batch Release to Finished Goods Inventory ​

The "Workbench" tab functions as your digital workshop dispatcher, tracking all batches actively undergoing cutting, beveling, stitching, or edge burnishing.

Once all 5 wallets pass final inspection:

  1. Locate the batch card on the workbench and click "Release Batch".
  2. Under-the-Hood System Mechanics:
    • 62.5 sq dm of leather, 20 m of thread, and finishing supplies are permanently deducted from inventory at their exact MAC valuation;
    • 5 completed wallets are transferred to finished goods inventory (the "Goods" tab) at their precise production cost of $55.25 per unit;
    • The batch card on the workbench closes and archives into completed records.

Scrap Mitigation: Logging Shop Floor Accidents

If a workpiece is damaged during stitching and a craftsman cuts replacement panels, record an inventory write-off in the "Materials" tab with the reason "Shop Floor Scrap". This maintains warehouse stock integrity without distorting the historical batch calculation.

Step 5. Retail Sales Processing ​

Finished stock is cataloged and ready for delivery to retail clients through your showroom, studio counter, or online storefront.

Open the "Sales" tab and click "New Sale":

  1. Selecting the Product: Pick "Bifold Wallet (Cognac)" from your showcase list. The system automatically selects stock lots using the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method.
  2. Assigning Packaging: Select the assigned packaging set (kraft gift box). Packaging stock is deducted from warehouse shelves simultaneously.
  3. Retail Pricing: The software applies the pre-configured 60% commercial markup, recommending $95.00 (you can fine-tune the final price or grant discounts if needed).
  4. Payment Method: Choose cash register receipt or direct electronic bank transfer.
  5. Click "Complete Sale". The transaction receipt is recorded, and the wallet is discharged.

Step 6. Financial Reconciliation & Ledger Closure ​

Completing the transaction triggers instant ledger adjustments across the workspace:

  • Cash Flow (DDS): An income entry of +$95.00 is generated and tied directly to the receipt identifier.
  • Transaction Margin: The system deducts the unit manufacturing cost ($55.25) and packaging expenditure ($3.50) from retail proceeds, recording the true net margin:
Financial Outcome
Net Commercial Profit per Transaction
Net Profit=$95.00−$55.25−$3.50=$36.25
  • Analytics: Revenue metrics, average transaction size, and breakeven horizons update automatically.
  • Owner's Equity (Capital): Finished stock asset value decreases by the unit manufacturing cost, while cash reserves expand by the full invoice amount. Total net worth grows by the exact net profit realized.

Consistent Workshop Cadence

The conveyor operates at peak efficiency only when transactions are entered promptly. Log raw materials on the day supplier crates arrive, and release completed runs from the workbench as soon as quality inspection clears. This eliminates inventory discrepancies and guarantees dependable financial oversight.
