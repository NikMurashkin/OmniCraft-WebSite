Production and Total Cost Formula ​

The classic manufacturing cost model powering each tool in this directory:

Engineering Model
Base Unit Production Costing Formula
Unit Cost=[Raw Materials×(1 + Scrap Rate)]+Components+Packaging+(Labor × Hourly Rate)+(Equip. Time × Depreciation)+Sales Tax
  1. Raw Material Base: Net weight or dimensions adjusted by the process loss coefficient (cutoffs, swarf, baking evaporation, purge towers).
  2. Components & Fittings: Fixed indivisible units (zippers, snaps, magnets, wicks, boxes, threaded inserts, labels).
  3. Direct Billable Labor: Actual bench work hours required multiplied by the target hourly craftsman rate.
  4. Equipment Depreciation & Overhead: Machine or tool runtime multiplied by the hourly wear and consumable tooling rate (blades, bits, nozzles, power), plus fixed per-order studio overhead.
  5. Sales / Revenue Tax (Ntax): Tax is assessed on the total selling price (gross revenue). A craftsman must never deduct tax from their personal hourly labor rate or clean net margin. Tax is factored into the retail price on top, forming the total landed cost (Stotal = Sprod + Ntax). In Step 5 "Taxes" of each calculator, this amount is reserved automatically.

From Standalone Calculators to Integrated ERP ​

An online calculator is an indispensable tool for fast quoting on custom commissions. However, as order volume expands, manually maintaining formulas creates three structural bottlenecks:

  • Price Drifts: Raw material prices rise while static spreadsheet estimates remain outdated.
  • Loss of Batch Costing: Material write-offs happen without reflecting moving average purchase costs (MAC).
  • Inventory Decoupling: Calculating an estimate on paper leaves physical raw material balances unchanged on the shelf.

This is why we built OmniCraft ERP — an offline-first desktop solution where formula costing connects directly to warehouse inventory, finished showcase items, and real-time cash flow.
